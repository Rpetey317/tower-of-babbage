Welcome to this short demonstration of live captions. At a conference, a speaker can move quickly between everyday words and technical names. Today, we will use Kubernetes as an example. Each stage sends audio to a small pipeline. The pipeline groups a few seconds of speech, asks a model for a transcript and a translation, and sends captions to the audience page. Kubernetes schedules each container on a machine with enough memory. If one container stops, another can start without changing the public address. We still watch latency carefully, because a correct caption that arrives ten seconds late is hard to follow. During this demo, listen for the words pod, scheduler, and translation. Notice the short pauses between ideas. Those pauses give the chunker a chance to cut the audio cleanly. Our goal is simple: people in the room should understand the talk as it happens, in the language they choose.
